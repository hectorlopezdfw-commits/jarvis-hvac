# Texas HVAC Operating System

Research and developer handoff  |  Version 1.0  |  October 20, 2026

Build a practical operating system that helps HVAC owners capture demand, deliver reliable work, understand job profit and collect cash. Start with a connected workflow from the first call through payment, then expand by evidence of value.

Recommended product decision


Launch an integration layer for Texas service and replacement contractors with approximately 3 to 15 field technicians. Keep existing dispatch and accounting systems authoritative where they work. Offer a simple native workspace only for contractors without usable software. Expand to commercial projects after proving the service workflow.

| Research inventory | Proposed operating model |
| --- | --- |
| 40 ranked weaknesses | Two solutions per weakness: a practical AI-assisted quick win and an integrated product workflow |
| 10 specialist agents | Each owns one operating category and a measurable work queue |
| 2 executive agents | CEO coordinates operating outcomes; CFO independently reviews financial effects |
| Controlled improvement | Learn from verified outcomes, test changes and release approved versions |

Build first


- Clean data, credential and consent controls before any automated write.

- Lead intake, valid booking, quote follow-up and documented closeout.

- Invoice readiness, job contribution and 13-week cash visibility.

- Exception queue showing evidence, owner, next action and financial effect.

The system cannot cover every possible problem or replace licensed judgment. It should detect known risks, flag uncertainty, route exceptions and improve under measurable controls. This package is a researched specification; it does not deploy agents or connect a contractor account.

Ranking method


Ranks 1 to 40 are an analyst priority order for the initial service/replacement segment. They weigh cash and contribution at risk first, frequency in the workflow second, then delivery capacity, adoption effort and time to demonstrate value. They are not measured statewide prevalence or statistically estimated losses. Safety, credential, consent, tax and data controls are launch gates regardless of rank.

Scope of coverage


Public research checked on October 20, 2026 across 30 cited sources. It covers common residential, commercial-service and construction workflow risks. No individual Texas HVAC company was audited, no interviews were conducted, and no statewide business list or market-size count was built. The requirements and solutions are design recommendations, not findings that every firm has each weakness.

One platform with different operating profiles

| Contractor profile | Priority configuration | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo and 1 to 2 technicians | Simple intake, job history, quoting, invoicing and owner brief | Minimal setup; avoid requiring multiple enterprise subscriptions. Most actions remain owner-approved. |

| 16 to 50 technicians and branches | Skills, roles, purchasing approvals and branch contribution | Stronger permissions, policy inheritance and delegated authority; dedicated onboarding. |

| Critical facilities and large enterprise | Customer-specific controls and incident procedures | Special engineering, security and uptime requirements need a separate validated deployment. |

Texas is not one service territory


Use configurable regional profiles. DFW may emphasize travel and rapid residential response; Gulf Coast operations may need more moisture, corrosion and storm workflows; rural and West Texas shops may need long-distance stock planning; El Paso-area deployments need the correct local time zone. These are discovery hypotheses to validate, not quantified regional findings.

What contractors may not know they need


Measure collected contribution rather than booked revenue; treat maintenance promises as capacity obligations; link repeat visits to original job cost; verify component dates and equipment identity; reconcile supplier credits and payment settlements; price the work with all direct delivery costs; prevent stale or conflicting records from driving automated actions.

1  Jobs look profitable but are underpriced


FIN | P1 | Finance Controller | Evidence context: S02, S10

Gap: Quotes omit loaded labor, travel, freight, financing fees, warranty exposure or supplier changes. Sales volume hides contribution loss.

Solution A: Use an AI-assisted cost worksheet with owner-approved labor burden and a weekly review of ten completed jobs.

Solution B: Build a deterministic price engine and margin guard that refreshes costs, explains quote variance and sends below-floor offers for approval.

Measure: Gross margin by job and quote-to-actual cost variance.

2  Missed calls and slow lead response


REV | P1 | Revenue Agent | Evidence context: S02, S22, S23

Gap: Technicians and owners cannot answer during work, after hours or during peak demand; paid leads disappear without a disposition.

Solution A: Configure existing AI overflow answering with a short service menu, human transfer and a daily missed-call review.

Solution B: Build bilingual intake that identifies the customer, checks territory and consent, handles approved safety scripts, reserves a valid slot and verifies booking.

Measure: Qualified lead response time and booking rate.

3  Cash shortages despite booked work


FIN | P1 | Finance Controller | Evidence context: S02

Gap: Receivables, supplier bills, payroll and customer deposits are viewed in separate places; profit is mistaken for available cash.

Solution A: Have AI explain a weekly 13-week cash worksheet; a bookkeeper reconciles cash and overdue balances.

Solution B: Build a CFO forecast with scenario ranges, collectible AR, deposit obligations, vendor due dates and minimum-cash alerts.

Measure: Forecast error, overdue AR and projected cash trough.

4  Wrong technician or inefficient schedule


OPS | P1 | Dispatch Agent | Evidence context: S01, S02

Gap: Dispatch ignores skill, location, parts, access windows, travel, promised service levels or overtime.

Solution A: Use a shared skill matrix and AI-drafted next-day schedule; dispatcher approves every move.

Solution B: Build constrained dispatch recommendations with live capacity, expiring slot reservations, realistic routes and customer-approved changes.

Measure: On-time arrival and contribution per available field hour.

# Texas compliance requirements

Translate verified requirements into reviewed product rules

| Area | Implementation requirement |
| --- | --- |
| TDLR credentials and scope | Store contractor class, endorsements, status, insurance, branch affiliation and technician credentials. Block assignments outside approved scope. Validate required license, company and department information on applicable documents and advertising. Track renewal and CE. [S03 S04 S05] |
| Local permits and inspections | Resolve the actual authority having jurisdiction using the service address, not ZIP alone. Store city-specific permit, adopted code, registration and inspection checklists. Fort Worth and Houston are examples only. An unknown city enters review. [S11 S12] |
| Refrigerants and equipment | Use current EPA source versions, equipment category, refrigerant, full charge, activity and component manufacture/import dates. The current table permits qualifying pre-2025 component inventory without an installation deadline; repair components differ from new systems. Never state a blanket ban on R-410A servicing. [S06] |
| Refrigerant records | Keep certification and service records appropriate to the actor and equipment. ER&R and Section 608 are distinct programs with different applicability. The 2026 transport exemption identified in this research is proposed, not a blanket final exemption. Have a specialist validate encoded thresholds and exceptions. [S07 S08 S09] |
| Sales tax | Classify residential versus nonresidential work, new construction, repair and documented periodic maintenance; contract structure and exemptions can change handling. Configure rules with a Texas tax professional. Do not apply one tax rate or taxable-base assumption to every HVAC job. [S10] |
| Incentives and marketing | Current Section 25C text excludes property placed in service after December 31, 2025. SECO says Texas HOMES/HEAR remained in design with no launch date as retrieved. Do not promise either as a current 2026 discount. Verify separate utility offers individually. [S19 S20] |

Regulatory entries need source URL, source excerpt, jurisdiction, effective date, review date, status, applicable facts, exceptions and a named human approver. A monitor can propose updates; it cannot publish new rules.

Each step produces a verified event or an exception


# Workflow from first call to collected cash

Each step produces a verified event or an exception

1 Intake


Capture caller intent, location, equipment if known, preferred language, contact permission and hazard signals. A gas/CO/fire/immediate danger signal invokes the approved safety script and immediate human/emergency routing; it is never treated as a routine diagnostic chat.

2 Qualify and reserve


Check territory, service scope, credential requirements, customer access, parts assumptions and real capacity. Create an expiring slot hold. Confirm only after the authoritative scheduler accepts the booking. On failure, provide a callback task rather than inventing a visit.

3 Estimate and authorize


Technician/estimator supplies facts. Revenue drafts from approved scope and price book. Finance calculates cost, fees and margin; Compliance validates required document fields and job classification. Customer accepts the immutable quote version before work.

4 Perform and document


Field staff complete work using licensed judgment. Record time, parts, measurements, photos, installed model/serial and approved scope changes. Offline work remains a draft until reconciliation; AI cannot fabricate missing evidence.

5 Verify and bill


Quality reviews required evidence; Compliance checks applicable requirements; Finance checks charges and tax. Each hold is released only by its designated qualified reviewer after verifying corrective evidence; manager approval alone cannot clear independent quality, compliance or finance holds. Any hold change invalidates prior action approvals. Create an invoice once using a business idempotency key and record accounting ID. Read back the result before marking invoiced.

6 Collect and reconcile


Use a hosted payment link. Record payment, fees, refunds and settlements separately. Reconcile the invoice balance to accounting. Customer Success initiates authorized service follow-up. CFO records collected contribution and monitors unpaid balances.

Design the failure path before enabling autonomous actions


# Exception workflows

Design the failure path before enabling autonomous actions

Callback and quality failure


Open a new visit linked to the original job and asset. Separate workmanship, new failure, access, parts and customer-use causes after human review. Quality creates corrective work and training proposals; Finance attributes the true rework cost. Do not automatically charge the customer or penalize the technician.

Supply and warranty


A parts recommendation needs model compatibility and a confirmed source. Reserve stock before dispatch; on shortage, generate alternatives for qualified review. Warranty packets include terms, serials, purchase/install dates and proof of work. A submitted claim is not a recovered credit; reconcile actual receipts.

Compliance update


A monitor detects an official source change, saves the source and opens a review. A qualified reviewer classifies proposed versus final requirements and approves a new effective-dated rule version. Regression tests run against historical and future scenarios. Active affected jobs enter an exception queue. Preserve the rule that governed prior decisions.

Storm or heat incident


Official alerts inform a dispatcher-reviewed incident mode. Apply service criticality, safe access, staff limits and customer commitments before margin. Notify affected customers through permitted channels. Safety alerts never become dynamic price increases. A human decides stop-work and return-to-service.

Disconnected vendor or uncertain write


Mark affected data stale and suspend dependent writes. Store the proposed action and request status. If a booking, message, invoice or PO times out, reconcile using the idempotency key or external reference before retrying. If status remains unknown, route to staff. A retry must not create a second booking or charge.

Unknown problem


Classify the issue as unsupported, preserve evidence, identify a human owner and deadline, and propose the smallest reversible next action. A novel event may add a reviewed issue type to the catalog; it cannot trigger automatic new code, a new agent or expanded permissions.

Independent improvement with controlled production releases


# How the system learns safely

Independent improvement with controlled production releases

Four distinct kinds of learning


| Type | Allowed mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Business memory | Store verified facts, preferences and approved SOPs with provenance and retention; do not treat an AI summary as a new business fact. |
| Workflow adaptation | Propose better schedules, reminders and task routing from measured outcomes. Test and release within owner-approved boundaries. |
| Statistical prediction | Fit demand or duration models when labels and sample quality are adequate. Track error, drift and uncertainty; keep simple baseline comparisons. |
| Product changes | Generate code, rule or prompt candidates in an isolated development workflow. Evaluation and human release approval are mandatory. No self-rewriting production. |

Improvement pipeline


- Observe: collect accepted/rejected recommendations, corrections, costs, delivery outcomes, callbacks and customer complaints. Preserve the original action version.

- Validate: filter noisy or malicious feedback. Human-confirm important labels. Separate tenants; no shared training on customer data without appropriate permission and rights.

- Propose: create a measurable hypothesis, affected cohort, expected benefit, harm metrics, sample plan and rollback trigger.

- Evaluate: use historical replay plus held-out cases for safety, privacy, consent, arithmetic, source fidelity, provider errors and normal work.

- Shadow: run without external effects and compare with trained staff and baseline. Release a small, authorized canary cohort only after passing gates.

- Promote or revert: a named owner approves the version. Monitor business and harm metrics; automatically suspend actions on serious regressions.

Avoid false improvement


A recommendation accepted by staff is not necessarily correct. A higher quote acceptance rate is not success if margin or callbacks worsen. Compare similar periods, job types and weather/capacity conditions. Holdout cohorts or stepped rollout help separate the product effect from seasonality. Record both benefit and harm. [S17]

The CFO dashboard must show uncertainty and reconciliation state

| Metric | Definition and decision use |
| --- | --- |
| Booking rate | Booked qualified inquiries / qualified inquiries in the same intake cohort; exclude duplicates and spam using reviewed rules. Track unserved demand separately. |

Realized-value ledger


Give each claimed benefit a source job or process ID, baseline, observed change, contribution calculation and reviewer. Time saved becomes cash benefit only if cost actually falls or capacity produces additional profitable work. Earlier collections improve working capital timing; they are not additional profit.

| Team and excluded costs | One technical lead/backend developer, one frontend/integration developer, fractional QA/design and HVAC operations expertise. Legal/tax review, vendor subscriptions, telephony, hosting, AI usage and support are additional. |

Monthly operating model


Tenant cost = fixed hosting allocation + AI requests x measured cost/request + voice minutes x measured cost/minute + messages x delivered-message rate + connector fees + storage/monitoring + support hours x support cost. Set per-tenant usage budgets and alerts. Publish included usage and overage terms; do not promise unlimited agents.

Illustrative contractor value calculation


Assume 10 genuinely recovered completed jobs per month at $250 incremental contribution each: $2,500. Add 12 hours of realized payroll savings at $30/hour: $360. Deduct a hypothetical $699 subscription and $150 incremental usage: $2,011 monthly net value. This is a scenario, not a forecast. If the hours do not reduce payroll or generate profitable work, exclude $360 and net value is $1,651. Do not count the same job gain twice.

Commercial packaging hypothesis


Test a narrow lead-to-cash package before selling the entire vision. Example price tests: $299/month for an owner-assisted basic workflow; $699/month for a connected small-team pilot; negotiated pricing for complex integrations and commercial work. These are discovery hypotheses, not established market-clearing prices. Setup/migration and required third-party plans must be visible.

Build versus configure decision


For an individual contractor, configuring existing tools may be the lower-cost solution. Build a proprietary OS only when repeated customer evidence shows a valuable gap, data access is achievable and subscription economics cover onboarding, support and usage. Compare against existing Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan capabilities before each module. [S21 S22 S23 S29 S30]

# Validation plan and open decisions

Turn research hypotheses into a tested operating product

Interview the workflow rather than opinions about AI


- Ask each owner to walk through the last missed inquiry, unprofitable job, callback, overdue invoice and supplier credit. Record time lost, financial effect and workaround.

- Ask dispatchers and field staff to demonstrate a difficult day, incomplete closeout and parts failure. Observe phone-only use and weak mobile connectivity.

- Ask bookkeepers to reconcile sales, payments, deposits, taxes and costs for the same sample jobs. Note the authoritative record and the largest missing field.

- Ask commercial managers about SLAs, site access, customer POs, approval delays, retainage and change orders. Do not assume a residential app fits these needs.

- Test willingness to pay for one quantified result with a reviewable pilot scope, total cost and cancellation/export terms.

Open decisions that affect implementation


Pilot cohort and existing FSM; required API approval; data quality and import rights; telephony and recording policy; languages; authority thresholds; security/retention requirements; applicable jurisdictions; payroll/accounting responsibility; onboarding capacity; pricing and support model; baseline and pilot evaluation design. Resolve these before committing a fixed-price full build.

Known coverage limits


Public sources establish legal/technical context and broad operating signals, not the prevalence or loss per weakness for every Texas contractor. The detailed priority order and both solutions per issue are analyst inferences requiring field validation. A 40-item inventory is deliberately broad but not exhaustive. Specialty industrial work, government procurement, union/franchise agreements, lien deadlines and regulated critical facilities need additional scoped discovery and professional review.

Developer instruction


Estimate E01 through E10 and E15 first. Demonstrate one tenant-isolated lead-to-cash flow using synthetic records, then a permissioned pilot dataset. Submit a connector-access checklist, data map, threat model, acceptance plan and phased quote. Do not start by building every specialist as an independent always-on chatbot.
